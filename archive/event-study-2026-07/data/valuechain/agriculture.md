# Agriculture & Food Value Chain: Chokepoints, Dependencies, and Trade-Policy Exposure

## 1. Critical Chokepoint Dependencies

**Potash (K)**: Extremely concentrated — Canada, Russia, and Belarus together account for roughly 60%+ of global production, with Canada alone >30%. Company-level shares: Nutrien ~20.7%, Chinese producer QSL ~15%, Mosaic ~12.8%, Uralkali (Russia) ~7.9%, Belaruskali (Belarus) ~7.5%. Two export cartels dominate logistics: **Canpotex** (Nutrien + Mosaic) and the **Belarusian Potash Company/BPC** (Uralkali + Belaruskali). The **US lifted sanctions on Belarusian potash in December 2025** (tied to a Lukashenko political-prisoner release deal), while the **EU has not** — creating a market bifurcation.

**Nitrogen**: Made via the Haber-Bosch process, natural-gas-intensive, so nitrogen fertilizer economics track gas prices directly. **CF Industries** is the key North American pure-play beneficiary of cheap shale gas (shares +59% in three months in late 2025/early 2026). China's urea/nitrogen exports fell >90% y/y in 2024 as Beijing prioritized domestic supply, tightening global nitrogen availability and lifting prices ~17% y/y.

**Phosphate**: Morocco (via state-owned OCP) has become the swing alternative supplier as China restricted exports and Russia faced sanctions friction — Morocco's phosphate export revenue rose ~19% in the first three quarters of 2025. Mosaic is the key US-listed phosphate name.

**Export logistics (Mississippi/Gulf)**: The Mississippi River basin handles ~92% of US agricultural exports and ~78% of global soybean/feed-grain exports; ~48% of corn/soybean exports move by barge; New Orleans-area Gulf terminals handle 43% of bulk ag exports.

**Black Sea corridor**: Ukraine/Russia together are dominant wheat/corn/sunflower-oil exporters; the war and blockade/corridor dynamics remain a live source of global wheat price volatility.

**Seeds/crop chemicals**: Bayer (post-Monsanto) and Corteva dominate US corn/soy seed — Corteva ~38% corn/~38% soy, Bayer ~33% corn/~28% soy, Syngenta (ChemChina-owned) ~9% soy. Top-4 firms control 50-60% of global seed and 60-70% of global pesticides.

## 2. Value Chain Structure

Inputs (seeds: Bayer, Corteva, Syngenta → fertilizer: Nutrien, Mosaic, CF Industries → equipment: Deere, AGCO, CNH Industrial) → Farming (fragmented, shock absorber) → Grain trading/processing (the **"ABCD" companies** — ADM, Bunge (public), Cargill, Louis Dreyfus (private) — historically ~90% of global grain trade; **Bunge completed an $8.2B merger with Viterra in July 2025**) → Export/shipping (Mississippi barge → Gulf terminals vs. Brazil's Santos/Paranaguá) → End markets (China largest soybean buyer, historically ~60% of US soybean exports pre-2018).

## 3. Downstream/Indirect Dependents

- ADM, Bunge feel volume/margin swings when China shifts purchases toward Brazil (both have Brazilian origination as a natural hedge).
- Deere/AGCO: China's retaliatory 10% tariff on US farm equipment (Feb 10, 2025) + Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs. Deere quantified ~$500-600M pretax tariff impact for FY2025.
- Farmer lawsuits allege Mosaic, Nutrien, CF Industries coordinated to keep fertilizer prices elevated.
- ADM/Bunge biofuel/crush margins indirectly exposed to both trade policy and biofuel policy.

## 4. Historical Bottleneck/Shock Events

1. **2018-2019 US-China trade war**: 25% retaliatory tariff on US soybeans; export losses $6.9-10.2B; China permanently shifted sourcing toward Brazil (2% → 71% of imports by 2024). USDA paid ~$8.5B in trade-aid.
2. **Feb 2022– Russia-Ukraine war**: Blocked Black Sea ports halted ~45M tonnes/year grain flow. Black Sea Grain Initiative (Jul 2022-Jul 2023) partially restored flows before Russia terminated it.
3. **2021, US sanctions on Belaruskali**: Tightened global potash supply, contributing to 2021-2022 fertilizer price spike.
4. **Feb 2025**: China's 10% tariff on US farm equipment hit Deere/AGCO/Caterpillar export sales.
5. **2025, "Liberation Day" tariffs + China's soybean slow-walk**: Despite Oct 2025 Trump-Xi summit deal, China only bought ~332,000 tonnes in two months after, relying on stockpiles and Brazil (~80% of China's 2025 soybean imports).
6. **July 2025**: Bunge-Viterra merger completion ($8.2B, ~$34B incl. debt).

## 5. Current Risk Points (July 2026)

- US-China ag tariff rollback "agreed in principle" as of early July 2026 (mutual 10% tariff removal on soybeans/corn/wheat/cotton/sorghum/pork/beef/dairy). Exports to China Jan-Mar 2026 up 57% y/y but volumes still below pre-trade-war levels.
- China's structural pivot to Brazil (71-80% of soybean imports) appears sticky — tariff "wins" may not fully restore volumes.
- Fertilizer sanctions geography split (US lifted Belarus sanctions, EU hasn't) — arbitrage/reallocation flows to track.
- Persistent nitrogen/phosphate cost inflation from elevated natgas + China export restraint (CF Industries beneficiary).
- Mississippi River low-water events (recurrent, most recently flagged Sept 2025) can amplify trade-driven export-volume swings.
