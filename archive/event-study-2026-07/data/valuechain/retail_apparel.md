# Retail & Apparel Value Chain: Chokepoints, Dependencies & Disruption Mapping

## 1. Critical Chokepoint Dependencies
- **Manufacturing concentration**: China (~31.6% of global apparel exports), Vietnam (#1 for Nike/Adidas footwear), India, Bangladesh (RMG = 84%+ of country's export earnings — single point of failure).
- **Cotton/UFLPA**: >90% of China's cotton from Xinjiang. Since June 2022, CBP reviewed 16,700+ shipments (~$3.7B); UFLPA Entity List now 144 entities (May 2024: +26).
- **Footwear**: Nike FY2024 — Vietnam ~50%, Indonesia ~27%, China ~18%. Adidas 2025 — Vietnam 41%, Indonesia 31%, China 14%.
- **De minimis elimination**: $800 threshold suspended for China/HK May 2, 2025; globally Aug 29, 2025. Shein US DAU -25%, Temu -52%; prices +20-40%. EU following (abolishing €150 threshold by 2026).

## 2. Value Chain Structure
Cotton/synthetic fibers → yarn/fabric mills (China/India/Vietnam/Bangladesh) → cut-and-sew garment factories → Brands (Nike, Adidas, VF Corp, Levi's, Lululemon, Gap, Under Armour, Columbia) + Retailers (Walmart, Target, TJX, Amazon, Costco) + DTC fast fashion (Shein, Temu) → consumers. Brands rarely own manufacturing — tariff cost falls on importer of record.

## 3. Downstream Dependents by Sourcing Mix
**Most exposed**: Target (China-heavy apparel/home/beauty), Nike (Vietnam ~44%), Columbia/Under Armour/Deckers/American Eagle, Shein/Temu (double-exposed to tariffs + de minimis).
**Diversifying but exposed**: VF Corp, Adidas, Lululemon (Vietnam-concentrated).
**Relatively insulated**: Walmart (China reliance cut 80%→60-70%, building Mexico/India alternatives), Gap (<10% China), Levi Strauss ("minimal" China exposure), TJX (off-price, buys via domestic middlemen).

## 4. Historical Bottleneck/Shock Events
1. **Aug 2019**: Section 301 List 4 tariffs first hit finished apparel/footwear (15%, reduced to 7.5% under Phase One Jan 2020).
2. **Aug-Dec 2021**: LA/Long Beach port congestion — up to $6.2B clothing shipments delayed.
3. **June 2022+**: UFLPA enforcement rolling entity-list additions.
4. **April 2, 2025 "Liberation Day"**: China 34%, Vietnam 46%, Cambodia 49% tariffs; Nike -12% intraday. April 9: 90-day pause to 10% blanket (ex-China).
5. **May-Aug 2025**: De minimis elimination (China then global) — Shein/Temu business model disruption.
6. **July 2, 2025**: US-Vietnam deal cut tariff 46%→20%; Nike/apparel shares rose, Lululemon stayed ~13% below pre-Liberation-Day.
7. **Feb 20, 2026**: Supreme Court strikes down IEEPA tariffs (6-3); Trump replaced with Section 122 "temporary surcharge" (capped 15%, max 150 days). Vietnam 46%→10%, Bangladesh 37%→10% overnight.

## 5. Current Risk Points (July 2026)
- **Section 122 expiration cliff — July 24, 2026** (imminent). USTR signaling fallback to 10-12.5% Section 301 on ~60 countries, possibly 15-25% surcharge on Vietnam (effective tariff 27%→42-52%). Live dated catalyst.
- Post-SCOTUS: future tariffs must route through narrower/capped/time-limited authorities → shorter-fuse, expiring measures with recurring renewal-driven volatility.
- India emerging as tariff-favored hub (18% locked in Feb 2026) vs China ~34% effective.
- UFLPA entity-list expansion remains standing tail risk, unpredictable.
- Bangladesh's single-industry economic exposure (84%+ export earnings from RMG).

**Trading heuristic**: on new tariff headline, cross-reference (a) country named, (b) each company's disclosed sourcing mix, (c) whether action is IEEPA/Section-122-style (fast, expiring, litigation risk) vs Section 301 (slower, more durable/persistent repricing).
