# Telecom Industry Value Chain: Chokepoints & Dependency Map

## 1. Critical Chokepoint Dependencies
- **Network equipment (RAN/core)**: Huawei leads global revenue share 31% (H1 2025), Nokia 13%, Ericsson 12%, ZTE 10%. Ex-China: Huawei 21%, Nokia 17%, Ericsson 16%. Top-5 RAN suppliers control 96% (concentration increasing, not fragmenting).
- **Submarine cables**: ~98% built/installed by 4 firms — SubCom (US, Cerberus-owned), Alcatel Submarine Networks (Nokia subsidiary), NEC (Japan), HMN Technologies/Huawei Marine (China). Hyperscalers now control ~90% of transatlantic capacity, ~71% global subsea fiber (Google owns/part-owns ~33 cables, Meta ~15, Microsoft 5, Amazon 4) — cable-cut events now hit GOOGL/META/MSFT/AMZN directly.
- **Satellite/spectrum (Starlink/SpaceX)**: SpaceX IPO'd Nasdaq **June 12, 2026** (ticker **SPCX**, priced $135, closed day-1 $160.95 +19%, largest IPO ever at $75B raised; ~$149.58 as of July 7, 2026). Starlink = 61-69% of SpaceX revenue ($11.4B of $18.7B 2025), 63% EBITDA margins vs 30-40% for traditional MNOs. **This makes SPCX a directly tradeable Musk/Starlink-headline proxy for the first time.**
- **Semiconductors**: Broadcom/Qualcomm (fabless) depend on TSMC; AI accelerator demand squeezing telecom-grade component supply into 2026 (optical-transceiver lead times 6wk→6mo).

## 2. Value Chain Structure
Semiconductors (TSMC, Broadcom, Qualcomm) → Network equipment (Ericsson, Nokia, Huawei/ZTE, Cisco, Samsung, Ciena) → Submarine cables (SubCom, ASN, NEC, HMN — increasingly hyperscaler-financed) → Terrestrial carriers (AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile) ←→ Satellite/alt-infra (Starlink/SPCX, Kuiper/AMZN, OneWeb/Eutelsat) → Enterprises/consumers/governments. Satellite layer merging with terrestrial via direct-to-cell (T-Mobile's T-Satellite w/ Starlink, launched July 23 2025, now also serves AT&T/Verizon customers).

## 3. Downstream/Indirect Dependents
- AT&T: $14B 5-yr Ericsson deal (Dec 2023), dropped Nokia entirely — single-vendor concentration risk. Verizon: Samsung ($6.64B 2020) + Ericsson, dropped Nokia years ago.
- Open RAN's "diversification" vision has largely failed — vendor lock-in increasing, amplifying single-company shock transmission.
- Rural/small US carriers: FCC Huawei/ZTE rip-and-replace mandate cost ballooned $700M→$1.9B→$4.6B; only 42% complete as of mid-2026.
- Starlink/SpaceX: both disruptor (rural/traditional satellite) and dependency (Ukraine, conflict zones) — SpaceX can unilaterally restrict access (Ukraine 2023, Crimea 2022).
- Hyperscalers (GOOGL/META/MSFT/AMZN) now bear direct capex/outage exposure to cable incidents via ownership stakes.

## 4. Historical Bottleneck/Shock Events
1. **2019-2020**: Secure and Trusted Communications Networks Act (Huawei/ZTE ban + rip-and-replace) — multi-year rural carrier reshuffling, still incomplete 2026.
2. **Feb-Mar 2024**: Red Sea cable cuts (4 systems: SEA-ME-WE 4, IMEWE, EIG, FALCON GCX) — disrupted ~25% Asia-Europe traffic; recurred Sept 2025.
3. **Oct 2023-Dec 2025**: Baltic Sea cable/pipeline sabotage series — 11+ cables damaged (Balticconnector, C-Lion1, Estlink 2, Elisa cable) via Chinese/Russian "shadow fleet" vessels.
4. **Dec 2023**: AT&T's $14B Ericsson deal drops Nokia.
5. **June 2026**: FCC AWS-3 spectrum re-auction — $3.57B raised; Verizon 88.5% of spend; **SpaceX won licenses for first time** (terrestrial-adjacent spectrum).
6. **June 12, 2026**: SpaceX Nasdaq IPO (SPCX) — converts sector's largest disruptor into tradeable equity.

## 5. Current Risk Points
- Submarine cable vulnerability structurally unresolved (scarce repair ships, slow attribution, reactive NATO/EU response).
- **SPCX now a live event-driven ticker** — Musk statements, Starlink outages, T-Satellite news, Ukraine access decisions, rumored T-Mobile tie-up speculation all transmit directly.
- RAN vendor concentration worsening — AT&T (Ericsson-locked), Verizon (Samsung/Ericsson-locked) carry elevated single-vendor shock exposure.
- 6G equipment lock-in forming early (Ericsson pushing non-Open-RAN architectures; Pentagon trying open-source to break duopoly).
- TSMC capacity is cross-sector chokepoint linking AI capex cycles to carrier network buildouts.
- LEO competition: Amazon Kuiper (~700 satellites by mid-2026, targeting 3,236) and Eutelsat/OneWeb (600+) as credible Starlink challengers/hedges.
